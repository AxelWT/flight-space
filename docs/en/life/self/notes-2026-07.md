# July Notes

## Communication

- Don't do anything that undermines a child's sense of security — educating a child is about giving enough security. — Duan Yongping
- By the same logic, you're more creative when you yourself feel secure.
- Normal flow: identify who raised the topic > show attention > if interested, ask for details > share with each other > interest fades and the conversation ends naturally.
- If the flow deviates, it may indicate that one or both parties have a strong emotion, or some bad habit is at play.

### The Essence of Communication

Communication has two layers: **information transfer + emotional connection**.

### The Normal Rhythm of a Conversation

You raise a topic → the other person picks it up (showing attention) → asks for details → you answer with details → they share a similar experience or viewpoint → loop.

This loop signals that the other person is interested in your topic. If they pick it up but don't expand on it, the conversation ends naturally.

Likewise, when they raise a topic, the flow is the same — the key is **two-way flow**.

### Choosing a Response Based on Interest

| Your state | Strategy | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Want to keep talking | Actively drive the rhythm | Ask questions + share related things to extend the topic |
| Interest waning but still willing to engage | Switch to resonance mode | Brief transition (e.g., "I know that feeling…"), share a related experience or feeling |
| Don't want to continue | Wrap up gently | Brief summary response (e.g., "I see" / "That sounds really tough") + smile/nod, let the topic cool naturally |

### Three Conversation Rhythms Compared

**Normal rhythm (spiral ascent, two-way flow):**
> A speaks → B asks/picks up → A answers → B shares related → A asks/picks up again → ...

**Monologue rhythm (one-way output, topic dies):**
> A speaks → B: "Oh." (or pivots to a long monologue about themselves) → A loses interest

**Interrogation rhythm (no sharing, like an interview):**
> A speaks → B keeps asking → A keeps answering → B keeps asking → ...

### Stop Doing List

- If the other person takes your topic but steers it entirely to themselves and never throws it back — they're not interested in your topic; you can stop talking.
- Understand that your original purpose is to **share**, not to force the other person to be interested.
- During a conversation, observe your own emotions, then observe the other person's.

## Economics

- GDP growth dropping from 8% to 5% may mean nearly 30% of people face employment difficulty — **a small drop in growth rate corresponds to a large amount of individual hardship.**
- When thinking about economic questions, avoid black-and-white, either-or thinking. The methodology to replace it is **weighing trade-offs**. Black-and-white thinking is dangerous because it ignores the compromises and costs in reality.

## Culture

- There's a saying: **Western societies are flat societies, Eastern societies are hierarchical societies.**
  - West: Everyone pursues surpassing themselves, unlocking their own potential — adventurers risk death to push limits, driven from within.
  - East: You pursue "moving up a rank" — essentially pursuing being stronger than others. But when the goal is being stronger than others, it gets exhausting, because people are complex and comparison never ends.
- The trap of pursuing "being above others" is that the standard is external, relative, and ever-changing. Whereas the standard of "surpassing yourself" is internal, absolute, and self-defined. The former is a zero-sum game; the latter is a positive-sum game.

## Science

- **Science is not a stance, not politics, and does not compromise for power** — it is objective facts and laws.
- Following science means using **mathematical reasoning and data quantification** to qualitatively and quantitatively describe problems, describe facts, and solve problems.
- The core spirit of science: **falsifiability** — a theory that cannot be falsified does not belong to the realm of science.
