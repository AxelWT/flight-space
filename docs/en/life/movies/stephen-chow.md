# Stephen Chow Movies

> If you don't have a dream, what's the difference between you and a salted fish? —— _Shaolin Soccer_

Stephen Chow is the king of comedy in Chinese cinema. His signature *mo lei tau* (nonsensical) style created a golden era of Hong Kong comedy that shaped a generation.

## Early Days · First Breakthroughs

<div class="movie-section">

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1990 | **All for the Winner** | 赌圣 | The film that established Stephen Chow's mo lei tau style. Broke Hong Kong box office records. |
| 1991 | **Fight Back to School** | 逃学威龙 | An undercover cop goes back to high school. The first of a beloved trilogy. |
| 1991 | **Tricky Brains** | 整蛊专家 | A professional prankster takes on his biggest gig. Pure, no-holds-barred comedy. |
| 1992 | **Justice, My Foot!** | 审死官 | A cunning lawyer takes on corrupt officials. Won Stephen Chow his first Best Actor award. |
| 1992 | **Royal Tramp** | 鹿鼎记 | Trash-talking harem-guarding Wu Xia classic. "My admiration for you is like the Yangtze River — endless." |
| 1992 | **King of Beggars** | 武状元苏乞儿 | From general's son to beggar clan leader. The fall is far, but the rise is greater. |

</div>

## Golden Age · Peak Comedy

<div class="movie-section">

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1993 | **Flirting Scholar** | 唐伯虎点秋香 | Number 9527's rise from servant to scholar. The film that gave Cantonese slang "koi yeh" and introduced a generations to "Little Strong" (cockroach).<div class="quote">"Life goes up and down so fast — it's too exciting."</div> |
| 1994 | **From Beijing with Love** | 国产凌凌漆 | A James Bond parody set in mainland China. A butcher who moonlights as a secret agent.<div class="quote">"You think it's the wind blowing? No, it's me moving."</div> |
| 1994 | **Hail the Judge** | 九品芝麻官 | A low-ranking magistrate battles corruption. Justice may be late, but it arrives.<div class="quote">"I want them ALL!"</div> |
| 1994 | **Love on Delivery** | 破坏之王 | A timid delivery boy learns to fight for love. Sometimes the bravest thing is just showing up. |
| 1995 | **A Chinese Odyssey Part One** | 大话西游之月光宝盒 | Time-traveling Monkey King saga. You came for the laughs, you stayed for the tears.<div class="quote">"There was a love before my eyes, but I didn't treasure it."</div> |
| 1995 | **A Chinese Odyssey Part Two** | 大话西游之大圣娶亲 | The conclusion to the epic. The final line "He looks like a dog" hits harder than any punchline. |
| 1995 | **Sixty Million Dollar Man** | 百变星君 | A spoof of *The Six Million Dollar Man* with absurd body-transformation gags. |
| 1996 | **The God of Cookery** | 食神 | A fallen culinary god fights his way back. There is no god of cookery — or everyone is.<div class="quote">"There is no God of Cookery. Or rather, everyone can be one."</div> |

</div>

|---|---|---|---|

<details>
<summary>🎬 Trivia · Recurring motifs in Stephen Chow's films</summary>

<br />

- **The Rooster Bowl** — The same patterned ceramic bowl appears in multiple films (*All for the Winner*, *Forbidden City Cop*, *The God of Cookery*, etc.). A beloved Easter egg for fans.
- **"Little Strong"** — A cockroach named in *Flirting Scholar* became the Cantonese slang for cockroach.
- **"Wang Choi"** — That same film gave dogs this universal name in Cantonese.
- **Ng Man-tat** — Stephen Chow's iconic comedic foil, they co-starred in over 20 films.
- **Buddha's Palm** — The signature move appears in *Kung Fu Hustle*, *A Chinese Odyssey*, and others.
- **Transformation gags** — Women playing grotesque characters (Karen Mok, King-Tan Yuen, Lee Kin-yan) became a hallmark.

</details>
