# Speaking Well — Ma Dong

> Fresh and interesting techniques for mastering the art of speaking

This book analyzes communication/speech from five dimensions: **Communication, Persuasion, Negotiation, Presentation, and Debate**.

---

## Communication — The Flow of Power

> Every sentence is a game of power. The inability to speak well ultimately comes from not understanding the situation. The clearest indicator is: **power dynamics**.

### Personal Insights

In communication, power lies with both parties, and attention must be paid to the flow of power. Guide the other person to express themselves (which is also a process of self-expression), and learn to listen. Also, pay attention to the **emotions** of both yourself and the other person during communication, and practice **empathetic expression**.

**Communicating with Extroverts**: Listen carefully, empathize when they express emotions, and find appropriately scaled humor to instantly build rapport (though this is harder to master).

**Communicating with Introverts / When a group is silent**: Break the ice actively, lower the other person's guard, reduce the pressure to speak. Tips: check trending topics/internet memes in advance for conversation material, avoid asking personal questions upfront, topics like weather and hobbies are good openers.

**Communication Taboos**:
> If you want to succeed at something, first think about how you would fail at it, then avoid that path. — Inverse thinking (Munger)

### Book Notes

**Delivering Bad News**: Be professional, not sympathetic; be kind, not apologetic; offer presence, not forced comfort.
- Compare: "Sorry, I messed this up" vs. "It's unfortunate this didn't work out" (use the latter if it's not your fault).

**Breaking Awkwardness at First Meeting**: Talk about names, share observations, don't pressure the other person. Don't demand information; share attitudes instead. Use self-deprecation to throw out topics, and address questions to the whole group rather than singling someone out.

**When Someone Asks to Borrow Money**: Don't focus on the amount first — ask about the reason: "Have you run into some difficulty recently?"

**The Trap of "Focusing on the Issue, Not the Person"**: Although we emphasize focusing on the issue, everyone has their own version of the facts, so all communication is objectively "about the person." The way to avoid this trap is to use a **"feeling disclaimer"** — "Sorry, this may not be the truth, but I can't help feeling this way..."

**How to Speak Without Offending**: When someone expresses emotions that need attention, focus on the "person."
- "That's amazing, I wish I had your talent" ✅
- "Don't be sad, it doesn't matter anyway" ❌
- "I know you put a lot of time and effort into this, and it's really a shame it turned out this way" ✅

**Expressing Disagreement**: Don't judge others' behavior; only describe how it affects you — "When you do this, it makes me feel..."

### Facing Your Own Emotions

Don't deny your emotions. A normal person's emotions should have stages — from not angry to somewhat angry to furious.

**The First Step in Emotional Communication**: Notice the emotion, release it in small amounts, give advance warning. For example, if someone's joke goes too far, say: "This joke makes me uncomfortable. If you keep this up, I'm going to get angry."

When you're in an emotional state, be brave enough to state your needs — "I'm feeling a bit sad right now, I need to step away."

### Dealing with Others' Emotions

**Mistakes**:
1. Fighting back, explaining, or refuting when attacked ❌
2. Staying completely silent ❌
3. "Calm down first" — invalidates the other person's emotions, causing more backlash ❌

**Correct Steps**:
1. **Psychological isolation**: When you notice someone is in an intense emotional state, calmly observe the high-frequency words in their speech to identify their emotional needs.
2. **Empathetic phrasing**: Restate their situation and needs, letting them know you understand their feelings. "Yes, this is my fault, and you have every right to be angry with me."
3. **Exit the emotional storm by seeking solutions**: If they still won't let go, first furrow your brows to signal, then state firmly and clearly: "I don't think you should say that about this point, but that's not the main issue — let's focus on finding a solution."

**The "Ant Carrying an Elephant" Apology**: If it's your fault, take as much responsibility as possible — even to an exaggerated degree. Critics who originally had hostile intent will find it hard to pile on and may even try to comfort you.

**Using Self-Deprecation for Self-Protection**: Revealing your weaknesses through self-deprecation prevents others from attacking you with them. Appropriately exposing weaknesses in private spaces can easily build trust with those around you.

### Common Scenario Techniques

**When Someone Self-Deprecates**: They are protecting themselves by lowering their posture. Never follow their self-deprecating line of thought — they can say it about themselves, but if you say it (or agree too readily), it's awkward.
- Approach: Empathize, share your own difficulties, find companionship in shared struggles. If all else fails, say "You're being too modest" or change the subject.

**When Someone Shows Off**: They may be in a mood to show off. Don't interrupt, let them finish, give a compliment or two — it's all quite entertaining.

**When Someone Tells a Joke**: Don't explain the joke. If you get it, smile knowingly. If you don't, ask about the punchline and then smile.

**Disagreeing in a Group**: Showing off by refuting or belittling others is foolish. When there's a conflict of opinion, focus on the issue, not the person. When you feel you're not being objective, add "I think/I feel" — "I think there's another aspect to this..."

**Rejecting "Reason Only"**: Appeal to emotions and interests — appealing to interests is far more effective than appealing to reason.

## Persuasion — Power Lies with the Other Party

### Insights

It's not about appealing to emotion and reason, but **appealing to emotion and self-interest**. Changing others is difficult. When their view shifts from "I hate this" to "It's not so bad" or even "You have a point," persuasion has already begun to succeed. Power lies with the other party, and success is largely beyond your control.

### Book Notes

- **Don't ask "why," ask "what happened"**: Asking "why" gives the other person room to make excuses; asking "what happened" lets them describe the situation.
- **Guide emotional release**: Be even more emphatic than your friend. People who are angry hate being ignored and most want someone to share their outrage. "That's absolutely outrageous, worse than..."
- **Persuading older, higher-status people**: "You always taught us that... is important, but in this case...", "You might not be fully aware, but I really have no choice..."
- **Appeal to external pressure**: Let the objective pressure come from an external source, creating a common enemy to avoid damaging relationships.
- **"Put them on a pedestal"**: Give them a compliment about their high standards and wisdom, then naturally make your suggestion.

## Negotiation — Power Lies with Both Parties

### Insights

Know yourself and know your enemy, and you will never be defeated.

### Book Notes

- **Negotiating a raise**: "Boss, I'd like to know what conditions an employee generally needs to meet to get a raise?" "If I get a raise, I'll put more energy into the project." (Paint a picture)
- **The anchoring effect**: Quoting first gives the other party a feeling of "winning." By anchoring a high price first, subsequent reductions feel like a gain.

## Presentation — Power Takes Form

Whether prepared or improvising, introverted or extroverted, everyone experiences some degree of nervousness.

- Accurately assess the real harm of mistakes during a presentation — don't overestimate the severity.
- Have an accurate self-assessment; introverted personalities have unique advantages.
- Use **storytelling techniques** to avoid blanking out.

---

## Debate — Power Lies with a Third Party

Quick reactions and judgment are basic skills in debate. The goal is to make the other party accurately aware of their own problems. Slow reactions can be improved with practice, but wrong reactions lead you astray — watch out for **trap questions** and **brainwashing tactics**.
